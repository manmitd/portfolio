A faster way to document a sewing project

3 min read · Skip to results ↓

As Lead Product Designer at Sewjo, I designed one of its most requested features: StitchLog, a quick and simple way to document a sewing project as you make it.

It came down to one idea: logging should be fast enough to do with fabric still under the needle. Documenting a project only works if it doesn't interrupt the project, and every extra screen between the machine and the note makes it less likely someone logs anything at all. So I built a structured project space, one-tap entries, and capture tools that keep sewists in their work instead of pulling them out of it.

Decision 01

Every project gets a home

A project is more than a note. It has measurements, a deadline, a plan. A blank note leaves people to remember what to record. A structured project gives every entry somewhere to belong.

Each project opens to a full overview: name, description, target date, priority, tags. Body measurements can adapt to what you're making. Add what you need, drop what you don't.

Decision 02

One tap, one note, back to sewing

Logging used to open a separate editor for every entry, which pulled people away from what they were making. Now, tapping the plus opens a compact card with title, body, and formatting inline, no full-screen detour. Preset titles beat the blank page, dictation is built in, and any entry flips into a checklist with one tap. Each note tags by phase (Planning, Sourcing, Cutting, Sewing) so the journal stays organized on its own.

Decision 03

Built for quick actions

The things sewists want to log usually live somewhere else: a photo of today's progress, a fabric in their Stash, an idea on a website. Switching apps to grab each one is exactly the kind of interruption StitchLog needed to avoid.

So I designed every capture as a quick action from the log itself. Quick Pick snaps a photo to your log, a saved material drops in from Stash, and the timer starts, each in a single tap. You can even grab inspo from the web without leaving the app, and it all lands in one place.

Results

  • 35% Increase in daily engagement after the StitchLog restructure
  • ~1,500 StitchLog entries logged each day
  • Structured projects: Sewists set up a real project space instead of facing an empty note.
  • Faster logging: Quick-action cards replaced the full-screen editor, cutting the effort to capture a moment.
  • Fewer app-switches: In-app capture, Stash imports, and link extraction keep sewists in one place.
